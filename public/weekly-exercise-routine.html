Weekly
Exercise Routine

Six days a week without the soreness that forces a skip day. Each muscle twice weekly at moderate volume — enough to grow, light enough to recover by the next session. Two modes: Standard for your everyday session, Full when you have the room.

Standard · ~45 min: compound + 3–4 isolations. Your everyday session and the default. Each muscle twice a week.

Cardio & Walking
Daytime walking breaks
Every day · whenever
Break up sitting with a few minutes of walking through the day. Low-intensity, restorative, zero interference with lifting — one of the highest-value habits here. The walk to the gym can double as your general warm-up.
After-dinner walk
Every day · evening
10–20 min easy. Blunts the post-meal blood-sugar spike and is gentle enough not to disturb sleep. Keep it relaxed — this is recovery, not training.
Weekly run
Day 7 · morning
Once a week (2× is the ceiling, not a target). Sits on the yoga day because there's no lifting to blunt. Run first, then the stability holds as your cooldown. If it ever leaves you flat for Day 1, ease the intensity.
Rule of thumb: keep hard cardio and lifting hours apart so they don't cannibalise each other. Easy walking has no interference — do it anytime, including right after a lift. Never run hard right before lifting; if same session is unavoidable, lift first.

The non-negotiables

  • Warm up before every compound. 15 min upper, 20–30 min legs. Hyperextensions + light pulldowns before deadlift; body-weight squats before squatting.
  • Warm-up sets aren't counted in the numbers below. Every set listed (e.g. 3 × 12) means working sets. On compounds, add 2 ramp-up sets first — ~10–12 reps at 30–40% of your working weight, then a lighter bridge set — before the working sets. So a compound listed as 3 sets is really 2 ramp-up + 3 working = 5 total. Ramp-up weight should feel genuinely easy; if it's near your working load it isn't a warm-up.
  • Default is 3 × 12 working sets. On recovery-focused days, keep the working sets but pull the weight back (leave more in reserve) rather than cutting sets. Managing load, not volume, is how you stay fresh.
  • Never train compounds to failure. 1–3 reps in reserve. Biggest single factor in not being too sore tomorrow — and in not getting hurt.
  • Failure only on the last set of isolations. Sparingly early on.
  • Pick a swap, don't wait. The movement pattern is what matters. Machine occupied → dumbbell or barbell version, same slot, keep the clock moving.
  • Same exercises for 2–3 months. Rotate grips and angles, not movements. That's how you see progression.
  • Auto-regulate, don't skip. Sore on its day? Drop a set or lighten it. Showing up beats the perfect session.
  • Rest 2 min on compounds, 30–60s on isolations. Supersetting isolations at one station (see the ⇄ pair tips) keeps the session tight without cutting rest per muscle.
  • Form over weight. Leave the ego at the door — a small tweak in form can drop the weight 50%, and that's fine. Diagrams here are schematic; check YouTube (Squat University, Jeet Selal, Flying Beast) for full form breakdowns.
  • Protein 120–140g/day. Sleep uninterrupted, no alarm if you can. Recovery is built outside the gym.
  • Cardio in the morning, separate from lifting. Running 2x/week max. Never skip lifting for cardio.
  • Omega-3 + vitamin D for joints. Log at least weekly to track progression.
